Overview

Wolp+ 2026 has two rankings of its own. Wolp+ is the formula ranking: a model's power rating for every FBS team, plus any conference dials. The Chili Poll is a weekly Top 25, ranked by hand. The model also rates what each team has earned (quality wins). It never sees the Chili Poll, and the Chili Poll never feeds into it.

The Chili Poll

Each week's ballot ranks 35 teams. The Top 25 is the headline; teams 26–35 are listed as Also ranked. Ranking 35 gives the comparisons on the Home page a real scale: a team at #31 is close to the Top 25, not simply "unranked". (Ballots before week 6 ranked 25 teams plus an unordered "just missed" list; those teams are shown as 26–30 in their saved order.)

The rankings

Chili Poll
An opinion. The hand-made weekly ballot.
AP
Public opinion. The Associated Press Top 25.
Wolp+
The numbers, with a point of view. The Model's power rating plus any conference adjustments set under Conference dials ▾ (currently: none). With no adjustments it is exactly the Model. The Results Map, Best/Worst, quadrants, Fraud watch and Under the radar use the Wolp+ rank; spreads, win chances, projections, Quality wins and the Scorecard always use the pure Model.
Model
The numbers. Teams ranked by power rating: points better than an average FBS team on a neutral field. The difference between two teams' ratings, plus the home edge, is the model's predicted margin. It picks winners better than the AP poll (see the Scorecard on Previous Rankings) and comes close to the best computer ratings: on 2023–25 games it missed by about 0.15 points a game more than ESPN's FPI and Sagarin, and beat 24 of 33 tracked systems.

Quality wins and Strength of résumé (SOR) are shown alongside as résumé stats, not rankings: every win and loss, each weighted by how good the opponent is. Quality wins judges opponents by Model rank; SOR judges them by their records alone.

Power rating, step by step

  1. Margins have diminishing returns. A win counts as 50 × tanh(margin ÷ 50) points, so a 14-point win counts about 14, a 28-point win about 25, and a 50-point win about 38.
  2. Close games count as narrow results. A game is close if it ended within 8 points unless the winner led by 17+ going into the 4th quarter (more than two scores). Close games count as a 3-point result whatever the final margin. A one-score game that wasn't really in doubt (a late rally against a big lead) counts as its final margin + 2; for example, leading 31–14 after three quarters and winning 31–24 counts as 9.
  3. Win bonus: every win gets 0.75 extra points (and the loser loses them).
  4. Each game is judged against expectations. Ratings are fitted so the margins they predict match the credited results. A blowout a team was expected to win barely moves it; a close loss to a much better team can raise it.
  5. Location: home teams get 2.5 points. At a neutral site, the team closer to the stadium gets part of that edge (1.0 × home edge × how much shorter its trip is). Any team traveling farther than its opponent gives up 1.0 point per 1,000 extra miles.
  6. FCS games count too: an FCS opponent is treated as a team rated 18 points below the FBS average. A comfortable win over an FCS team barely moves a rating, but a narrow one lowers it, and a loss lowers it a lot.
  7. Openers count a bit less: each team's first game counts 0.8 of a game.
  8. Recent games count a bit more: each week further back, a game counts 0.94 as much, so a team's current form outweighs September.
  9. Preseason start: a mix of last season's rating, the share of production returning, and roster talent (weights fitted on earlier seasons), plus 5 points for Power 4 teams (stats from weaker conferences tend to overrate teams). Teams new to FBS start at the 10th percentile. Army, Navy and Air Force count as average in roster talent: they rank last in recruiting every year but play far better than that (on 2024–26 replays they beat the model by about 3 points a game until this fix). The start counts as 3 games before the season and fades to nothing by week 18.
Not used: betting lines (kept out so the model can disagree with them), per-play efficiency (it didn't improve predictions), and the Chili Poll. On a week-by-week replay of 2024–2026, the model's predicted margins were off by about 12.35 points per game on average; the closing betting line was off by about 12.0. That's a little behind the best computer ratings, such as ESPN's FPI and Sagarin, and ahead of most others.

Quality wins (résumé stat)

  • Opponent strength (0 to 1): from the opponent's Model rank: #1 is 1.0 and the last FBS team is 0.0. It always uses the pure Model, so the conference dials don't change it. With the dials at 0 this is the same left-to-right position as the opponent's dot on the Results Map, so a row of dots far to the right means a high Quality wins score.
  • A win is worth the opponent's strength × location (road 1.02, neutral 1.01, home 1.00), × (1 + 0.1 × strength) if it wasn't close.
  • A loss costs 0.5 × (1 − strength) × location (home 1.05, neutral 1.00, road 0.95), × 0.6 if it was close, and always at least 0.1. Losses weigh less than wins.
  • FCS: a win doesn't count; a loss counts -1.5.
  • Score = the total value of the games that count, divided by 12 (a full regular season). Every win adds and every loss subtracts, so beating a weak team can never lower the score, and more good wins mean a higher score. Example: a decisive home win over a 0.70-strength team is worth 0.70 × 1.00 × 1.07 = 0.75.

Strength of résumé (SOR)

  • Scored exactly like Quality wins (same win and loss values, location, close-loss and FCS rules, ÷ 12), with one difference: opponent strength comes from results only, never the power rating.
  • Opponent strength (0 to 1): each team gets a rating from its wins and losses alone, adjusted for who it played (the Colley method, one of the old BCS computers): no margins, no power rating, no preseason. An average team is about 0.5. After four weeks, 4–0 teams rate from about 0.6 to 0.9 depending on who they beat, 2–2 teams about 0.35 to 0.55, and 0–4 teams about 0.15.
  • Example: UConn lost at home to Maryland (2–2, strength 0.49): −0.5 × 0.51 × 1.05 = −0.27. Under Quality wins the same loss costs −0.26, because Maryland is Model #70 (strength 0.50). The two differ most for teams whose record and power rating disagree, for example a 4–0 team that won close games against weak opponents.

Win chances and projections

  • Win chance: a game's result typically lands about 16 points from the model's spread in either direction, so the spread converts to a win chance on a normal curve: −3 is about 57%, −7 about 67%, −14 about 81%, −21 about 91%. These percentages were the best-calibrated on 2024–26 replays.
  • Projected records: the rest of the regular season is simulated 10000 times using those win chances. In each simulated season, every team's true strength is also nudged by about 5 points, because a rating after a few games is itself uncertain (this matched 2024–25 final records best). The results give each team's expected record, its chance to finish unbeaten, to win 10+ games, and to reach 6 wins (bowl eligible).
  • Tested and not used: giving each team its own "x-factor" for how much it swings. A team that over- or under-performed a lot early was no more likely to do so later (correlation about 0), and team-specific swings made the win chances slightly worse.

Results Map

  • Each row shows a team's games, placed left to right by the opponent's rank in the metric chosen under X axis: Wolp+ (the default), FPI, SOR or Quality wins (#1 at the far right; FCS opponents at the far left). Best and Worst follow the same choice.
  • Green = win, red = loss. Dot size = margin (1–8, 9–16, 17+). Gold ring = close game; double ring = overtime.
  • A small number beside a dot is the opponent's Chili Poll rank. Best marks the win over the highest-ranked opponent (the rightmost green dot) and Worst the loss to the lowest-ranked one (the leftmost red dot).
  • Dots for similarly ranked opponents are stacked slightly above and below the line so none are hidden.
