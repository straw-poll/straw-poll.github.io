Voting

  • Anyone can sign in with Google; the site owner approves who can vote.
  • Each week, rank exactly 25 teams on the Vote tab. A team you leave off counts as #26.
  • Voting opens Sunday morning, once the previous week's games are final, and closes Tuesday at 11:59 PM ET. You can change your ballot as often as you like until then.
  • A ballot sent after the deadline (within 7 days) counts only if the owner approves it.
  • The poll averages everyone's ranks after dropping the most extreme ones for each team, and a team must be on at least 2 ballots (and a quarter of them) to appear. Full rules

Who sees your ballot

  • Before the deadline: only you.
  • After the deadline: the site owner, who compiles the poll, and (once leagues open) the members of your leagues. Nobody else; only the combined poll is public.

Pick'em

Coming with leagues. Your ballot doubles as a set of picks: in every game involving an AP Top 25 team, it picks the team you ranked higher. Each week you're scored against the AP poll's own picks on the same games, and the standings show how far ahead of or behind the AP you are. A confident pick (two teams far apart on your ballot) gains more when right and costs more when wrong. The exact scoring isn't published.

Overview

Straw Poll 2026 has two rankings of its own. Wolp+ is a power rating for every FBS team, built from game results. The Straw Poll is a weekly Top 25 combined from members' ballots. The model also rates what each team has earned (quality wins). It never sees the Straw Poll, and the Straw Poll never feeds into it; Straw+ is the one place the two are blended.

The Straw Poll

Every approved member submits a 25-team ballot each week, from Sunday morning (once the previous week's games are final) until Tuesday 11:59 PM ET. Nobody can see anyone else's ballot; only the combined poll is published, on Wednesday morning.

  • Combining: each team's ranks across all ballots are averaged, with a team left off a ballot counting as #26. The most extreme ranks for each team are set aside first, so one unusual ballot can't swing a team.
  • Who can appear: a team must be on at least 2 ballots, and on a reasonable share of them, so a single voter can never put a team in the poll.
  • Ties go to the team on more ballots.
  • Also receiving votes: the next ten teams by the same method.

Weeks 1–5 are the site owner's own rankings from before ballots opened; those listed 35 teams, with 26–35 shown as Also ranked.

The rankings

Straw Poll
Members' weekly ballots, combined.
Straw+
Each Straw Poll team's Wolp+ rating moved 30% of the way toward the rating that its Straw Poll rank would carry in Wolp+ (the rating of Wolp+'s team at that rank). The crowd sets the order it leans toward; the model keeps the spacing. Teams outside the Straw Poll keep their Wolp+ rating.
AP
The Associated Press Top 25.
Wolp+
Teams ranked by the Model's power rating: points better than an average FBS team on a neutral field. The difference between two teams' ratings, plus the home edge, is the model's predicted margin. It picks winners better than the AP poll (see the Scorecard on Previous Rankings) and comes close to the best computer ratings: on 2023–25 games it missed by about 0.15 points a game more than ESPN's FPI and Sagarin, and beat 24 of 33 tracked systems.

Quality wins and Strength of résumé (SOR) are shown alongside as résumé stats, not rankings: every win and loss, each weighted by how good the opponent is. Quality wins judges opponents by Model rank; SOR judges them by their records alone.

Conference Modifier ▾ (Research tab): a just-for-fun what-if. Add points to a conference to see how the rankings would change. It only changes your screen, never the real ratings.

Power rating

Wolp+ rates each team by how many points better or worse than an average FBS team it would be on a neutral field. The ratings are fitted to every result this season, so each game is judged against what was expected given both teams' strength. It accounts for:

  • The opponent: beating a strong team counts for more than beating a weak one, and a close loss to a much better team can raise a rating.
  • The margin: bigger wins count for more, with less and less extra credit for running up the score. Close games, and late scores in games that weren't really close, are treated with care.
  • Where it was played: home field, neutral sites and travel.
  • Games against FCS teams: they count, but a comfortable win barely moves a rating.
  • Recent form: recent games count somewhat more than early-season ones.
  • A preseason starting point: based on last season, returning players and roster talent, which fades out as the season's results come in.
Not used: betting lines (kept out so the model can disagree with them), per-play efficiency (it didn't improve predictions), and the Straw Poll. On a week-by-week replay of 2024–2026, the model's predicted margins were off by about 12.35 points per game on average; the closing betting line was off by about 12.0. That's a little behind the best computer ratings, such as ESPN's FPI and Sagarin, and ahead of most others.

Quality wins (résumé stat)

  • Adds up a team's wins, each worth more the better the opponent, and subtracts its losses, each costing more the weaker the opponent. Wins count for more than losses cost.
  • Opponent strength comes from the opponent's Wolp+ rank: the same left-to-right position as its dot on the Results Map (Wolp+ x axis), so a row of dots far to the right means a high score.
  • Road wins count slightly more, decisive wins slightly more, and close losses cost less. Wins over FCS teams don't count; losses to them cost a lot.
  • Beating a weak team can never lower the score.

Strength of résumé (SOR)

  • Scored the same way as Quality wins, with one difference: opponent strength comes from results only (each team's wins and losses, adjusted for who it played), never from the power rating.
  • The two differ most for teams whose record and power rating disagree, for example an unbeaten team that won close games against weak opponents.

Win chances and projections

  • Win chance: the model's spread is turned into a win chance based on how far real results usually land from it. A field goal favorite wins a bit more than half the time; a three-touchdown favorite almost always does.
  • Projected records: the rest of the regular season is simulated thousands of times from those win chances, allowing for the fact that early-season ratings are themselves uncertain. That gives each team's expected record and its chances to finish unbeaten, win 10 games, or reach bowl eligibility.

Results Map

  • Each row shows a team's games, placed left to right by the opponent's rank in the metric chosen under X axis: Wolp+ (the default), FPI, SOR or Quality wins (#1 at the far right; FCS opponents at the far left). Best and Worst follow the same choice.
  • Green = win, red = loss. Dot size = margin (1–8, 9–16, 17+). Gold ring = close game; double ring = overtime.
  • A small number beside a dot is the opponent's rank in the X axis metric, shown only for opponents in that metric's top 25 (on the Vote tab, it is the opponent's Straw Poll rank). Best marks the win over the highest-ranked opponent (the rightmost green dot) and Worst the loss to the lowest-ranked one (the leftmost red dot).
  • Dots for similarly ranked opponents are stacked slightly above and below the line so none are hidden.
