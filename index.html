Overview

Wolp+ is a weekly college football Top 25, ranked by hand. Alongside it, a model rates every FBS team two ways: how good a team is (the power rating) and what it has earned (quality wins). The model is a reference: it never sees the Wolp+ rankings, and the Wolp+ rankings never feed into it.

The Wolp+ rankings

Each week's ballot ranks 35 teams. The Top 25 is the headline; teams 26–35 are listed as Also ranked. Ranking 35 gives the comparisons on the Home page a real scale: a team at #31 is close to the Top 25, not simply "unranked". (Ballots before week 6 ranked 25 teams plus an unordered "just missed" list; those teams are shown as 26–30 in their saved order.)

The three ratings

Power rating (Model)
How good a team is. Points better than an average FBS team on a neutral field. The difference between two teams' ratings, plus the home edge, is the model's predicted margin.
Quality wins (QW)
What a team has earned. Its wins and losses, each weighted by how good the opponent's record is. Results only: no scoring margins and no power ratings.
Composite
A blend of the two (currently ), set under Composite ▾ in the header.

Power rating, step by step

  1. Margins have diminishing returns. A win counts as 50 × tanh(margin ÷ 50) points, so a 14-point win counts about 14, a 28-point win about 25, and a 50-point win about 38.
  2. Close games count as narrow results. A game is close if it ended within 8 points unless the winner led by 17+ going into the 4th quarter (more than two scores). Close games count as a 3-point result whatever the final margin. A one-score game that wasn't really in doubt (a late rally against a big lead) counts as its final margin + 2; for example, leading 31–14 after three quarters and winning 31–24 counts as 9.
  3. Win bonus: every win gets 0.75 extra points (and the loser loses them).
  4. Each game is judged against expectations. Ratings are fitted so the margins they predict match the credited results. A blowout a team was expected to win barely moves it; a close loss to a much better team can raise it.
  5. Location: home teams get 2.5 points. At a neutral site, the team closer to the stadium gets part of that edge (1.0 × home edge × how much shorter its trip is). Any team traveling farther than its opponent gives up 1.0 point per 1,000 extra miles.
  6. FCS games: wins over FCS teams are ignored; a loss to an FCS team counts as a loss to a team rated 15 points below the FBS average.
  7. Openers count a bit less: each team's first game counts 0.8 of a game.
  8. Preseason start: a mix of last season's rating, the share of production returning, and roster talent (weights fitted on earlier seasons), plus 5 points for Power 4 teams (stats from weaker conferences tend to overrate teams). It counts as 3 games before the season and fades to nothing by week 14.
Not used: betting lines (kept out so the model can disagree with them), per-play efficiency (it didn't improve predictions), and the Wolp+ rankings. On a week-by-week replay of 2024–2026, the model's predicted margins were off by about 12.5 points per game on average; the closing betting line was off by about 12.0.

Quality wins

  • Opponent strength (0 to 1): a results-only rating (the Colley method) from wins and losses, adjusted for who each team played. An average team is about 0.5.
  • A win is worth the opponent's strength × location (road 1.02, neutral 1.01, home 1.00), × (1 + 0.1 × strength) if it wasn't close.
  • A loss costs 0.5 × (1 − strength) × location (home 1.05, neutral 1.00, road 0.95), × 0.6 if it was close, and always at least 0.1. Losses weigh less than wins.
  • FCS: a win doesn't count; a loss counts -1.5.
  • Score = the average value of the games that count. Example: a decisive home win over a 0.70-strength team is worth 0.70 × 1.00 × 1.07 = 0.75.

Composite

Each team's power rating and quality-wins score are turned into percentiles among FBS teams (0 = lowest, 1 = highest). The Composite is the blend of the two, then ranked. The saved blend is what everyone sees; moving the slider changes only your own view until the page is reloaded.

Results Map

  • Each row shows a team's games, placed left to right by the opponent's Composite rank (#1 at the far right; FCS opponents at the far left).
  • Green = win, red = loss. Dot size = margin (1–8, 9–16, 17+). Gold ring = close game; double ring = overtime.
  • A small number beside a dot is the opponent's Wolp+ rank. Best marks the win over the highest-ranked opponent (the rightmost green dot) and Worst the loss to the lowest-ranked one (the leftmost red dot).
  • Dots for similarly ranked opponents are stacked slightly above and below the line so none are hidden.

Home page

Fraud watch
Teams in the AP Top 25 that both Wolp+ and the Composite rank at least 8 spots lower. A team outside the Wolp+ 35 counts as #36. Sorted by the size of the gap.
Under the radar
Wolp+ teams that both Wolp+ and the Composite rank at least 8 spots higher than the AP. A team outside the AP Top 25 counts as AP #30.
Model spread
The model's predicted margin, written like a betting line (favorite −points, rounded to the half point). It never uses the betting line.
Line
The median spread across sportsbooks, from CollegeFootballData.com.
Upset alerts
Games hand-picked each week, usually where the model favors the betting underdog or disagrees with the line by a touchdown or more.
Big games
This week's games with a Wolp+ ranked (1–35) or AP Top 25 team where the favorite is giving 14 points or less (by the betting line, or the model spread when there's no line). When both teams are ranked (Wolp+ or AP), the limit is 21 points.

Expected points added per play on offense, and allowed on defense (garbage time excluded). Defense: lower is better.

Success rate (share of plays that keep a drive on schedule) and explosiveness (size of successful plays).

Defs / Offs faced
Strength of schedule: the average adjusted EPA of the FBS defenses (lower = tougher) and offenses (higher = tougher) a team has played.
▲ ▼ NEW

H2H, LOOP
Workspace reminders: a head-to-head result that disagrees with the order, or teams that beat each other in a circle.

Data and updates

Team data comes from CollegeFootballData.com. Results, stats, ratings and the AP poll update every Sunday morning; the schedule and betting lines refresh again on Thursday. Only FBS teams are rated.
